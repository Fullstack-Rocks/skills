## How the site uses these files

- The wordmark, emblem and arrow are inlined into `sites/estate/*.html` as `aria-hidden` SVG so they inherit `currentColor` (ink in the header, paper in the footer and invitation, clay in links, system colours in forced-colors mode). The wordmark's accessible name comes from its link: “Orrisvane House — home” in the header, “Orrisvane House” in the footer.
- The site serves its own copies of `chair-condition.webp`, `chair-intervention.webp`, `chair-finished-detail.webp`, `breakfast-table.webp`, `west-room-library.webp` and `estate-plan.svg` from `sites/estate/assets/`. The three chair copies show one walnut spindle-back armchair on the atelier bench from one viewpoint: the rear seat joint opened with its pale tenon exposed; two hands steadying the leg and working a fine tool into that joint; the joint closed with old scratches and patina kept. The alt text above matches those copies.

## Completed photograph generation

The three photographs above (`orchard-walk.webp`, `chair-finished-detail.webp`, and `returning-books.webp`) were generated with Nano Banana via `gemini-api image` at `2K` (`3:2` and `4:5`), resized with `sips` to their target pixel dimensions, and encoded with `cwebp -q 82`. Each is under 400 KB. Existing photographs supplied lighting, palette, grain, room-material and subject-continuity references; none was extracted from `design.jpg`.

Prompt set: photographer's briefs for Orrisvane House describing a guest walking among mature olive trees in soft afternoon light (`orchard-walk.webp`), the same walnut writing chair's rear rail-to-stile joint after conservation on the atelier workbench (`chair-finished-detail.webp`), and a dovetailed walnut belongings box beside well-used unbranded books on the oak library shelf (`returning-books.webp`). All prompts kept the estate, guests, and walnut chair consistent and required natural colour, fine photographic grain, and no text, logos, watermarks or real brands.
