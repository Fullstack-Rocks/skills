## 1. **Name and house**

**Maison Talvrenne**  
**Based in:** Fleurier, Val-de-Travers, Switzerland.

*Prepared from Maison Lumen’s brief, all four research documents, and web research using competitors’ official sources. Talvrenne, its people, products and operating arrangements are fictional. Files changed: none.*

Restoration watchmaker **Alix Talvrenne** founded the house in 2016 with movement constructor **Éline Dervaillet**, after years spent repairing watches whose original technical records had disappeared. They began with a question: what should a watchmaker leave for the person who will repair the watch next? Four years of development produced their first manually wound calibre, with individually removable bridges and a complete service record accompanying every watch. The founders still own the business, and their 24-person workshop now completes approximately 240 watches annually. They maintain one core movement platform, directing development toward finishing, reliability and repair rather than a calendar of new complications.

## 2. **What they make**

The flagship is the **Talvrenne T-01**, a small-seconds wristwatch made for regular wear and close examination.

- **Case:** stainless steel, 37.5 mm diameter, 8.8 mm thickness and 44 mm lug-to-lug; sapphire front and back; individually pressure-tested to 5 bar.
- **Movement:** house-designed, manually wound calibre T/01; conventional Swiss lever escapement, 3 Hz frequency, 60-hour power reserve and stop-seconds setting.
- **Finishing:** German-silver bridges with hand-rounded bevels, four inward corners finished by hand, black-polished steel fittings and circular graining beneath the dial. A finish map identifies both handwork and machine operations.
- **Dial:** matte silver or charcoal, complete minute markings, recessed seconds at six and polished steel hands. Personalisation is restricted to strap length and caseback engraving.
- **Production and ownership:** approximately 240 pieces across both dial versions each year, priced at **CHF 48,000 before tax**. Every watch includes its serial-specific inspection dossier and technical repair pack.

The workshop machines its plates, bridges and train wheels. Specialist partners supply cases, crystals, jewels, springs and straps; their contributions are disclosed in the provenance record.

## 3. **Niche and audience**

**The technically literate collector.** Typically 45–65, this buyer has built a considered collection over fifteen years and already owns a Patek Philippe dress watch, an A. Lange & Söhne and perhaps a Laurent Ferrier. They understand finishing well enough to ask what happens beneath the dial. Talvrenne earns their attention through inspectable work, accessible technical explanations and a service arrangement they can examine before committing. They buy a watch to wear and study, with greater interest in the movement’s construction than its auction prospects.

**The design patron buying a first independent.** Typically 35–55, this buyer collects furniture, ceramics or photography and already wears a Cartier Tank or Rolex Oyster Perpetual. They appreciate proportion and material before they learn calibre terminology, and want a direct relationship with the people making their next significant purchase. Talvrenne offers a compact watch that works with their everyday wardrobe, a welcoming bench appointment and clear answers about cost, delivery and maintenance. The ownership documentation makes an unfamiliar independent house easier to trust.

## 5. **What sets them apart**

1. **The owner receives evidence about their actual watch.**  
   Every serial has unretouched movement photographs, including hidden surfaces before assembly; a map of finishing operations; and named assembler and inspector sign-offs. **Proof a sceptic can examine:** a complete sample dossier, inspection of the corresponding watch under magnification, and its fifteen-day cased-watch test report showing positions, winding states, results and acceptance criteria.

2. **Repair knowledge travels with the watch.**  
   The owner receives assembly drawings, a parts catalogue, material specifications, lubrication instructions and permission for qualified independent repairers to use them. Service-critical spares are stocked against a written 25-year commitment, supported by a reserve funded from each sale. **Proof a sceptic can examine:** the supplied documents, reserve-account verification, spare-parts inventory and a recorded overhaul completed by an independent watchmaker using the pack.

3. **A stable calibre receives sustained attention.**  
   Both dial versions share the same movement and service parts; changes are recorded by revision, with compatibility stated explicitly. Production planning reserves workshop time for existing owners’ watches. **Proof a sceptic can examine:** the calibre revision history, parts-compatibility table, annual production ledger and published service turnaround figures.

The defensibility lies in maintaining these practices over years: they require documentation labour, inventory funding and restraint in product development.

## 6. **Voice**

**Five adjectives:** precise, composed, candid, tactile, welcoming.

| Do | Don’t |
|---|---|
| Name the material, operation and person responsible. | Substitute “exceptional craftsmanship” for an explanation. |
| Explain technical terms in ordinary language. | Use specialist vocabulary to test whether a visitor belongs. |
| State production capacity, delivery windows and service terms plainly. | Manufacture urgency through countdowns or mysterious allocation language. |

**Sample headlines**

- “Every bevel has a record.”
- “Made with the next watchmaker in mind.”

**Sample paragraph**

> The T-01’s bridges are cut in our Fleurier workshop, then bevelled and polished by hand. Before assembly, we photograph the surfaces that will disappear beneath the dial. Those photographs accompany your watch, together with its rate results and service drawings. At your appointment, we will open the dossier beside the watch and look at both with you.

Calculated contrast: graphite on paper **13.2:1**; slate on paper **6.0:1**; paper on bench green **8.5:1**. Mineral rule exceeds **3:1** against both principal surfaces.

**Typography:** [Newsreader](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Newsreader) 400 for editorial headings and occasional italic passages; [Public Sans](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans) 400/500 for body copy, navigation and specifications. Body text begins at 16 px with 1.6 line-height. Hierarchy comes from scale, spacing and placement.

**Imagery and art direction:** an inspection table translated into an editorial website. Pair a complete watch in soft daylight with tightly framed movement studies under directional light. Show real working gestures, tools and named craftspeople. Use matching views of components before and after finishing, with factual captions. Combine 4:5 portraits and 16:9 bench photographs with a fine-line exploded calibre drawing. Develop an original wordmark and bridge-derived monogram; retain text labels beside technical icons.

Use an asymmetric twelve-column grid, generous margins, square image edges and solid text panels beside photographs. Product and craft images carry useful alt text; all copy remains selectable text.

**Motion:** restrained 400 ms underline transitions and optional 600 ms image dissolves. Keep native scrolling and pointer behaviour. Any workshop film starts on request and has visible controls; reduced-motion preferences disable transitions and retain still imagery. Keyboard focus must remain immediate and conspicuous.

## 8. **The website**

**Pages:** Home; T-01; The Workshop; Ownership & Service; Appointment. Privacy and accessibility information sit in the footer. Primary navigation contains four links: **The Watch · The Workshop · Ownership · Appointment**.

**Homepage sections, in order:**

1. **The watch and the premise.** Complete T-01 portrait beside “Made with the next watchmaker in mind,” a plain category description and the appointment action.
2. **The calibre.** Reverse-side photograph, numbered bridge diagram and a concise specification ledger.
3. **The work under magnification.** Three finishing details, each identifying an operation and linking it to the sample dossier.
4. **The people at the bench.** Alix, Éline and the workshop team; founding dates and disclosed supplier contributions.
5. **The record that accompanies it.** Sample photographs, inspection signatures and regulation results.
6. **Ownership over time.** Repair documentation, spare-parts provision and the service process.
7. **A private bench appointment.** What visitors can handle, inspect and discuss; repeat the single primary action.

The T-01 page introduces materials and construction before specifications, then quietly states the price and current delivery window. Appointment requests are welcome from first-time buyers; visitors need no collector credentials.

**The one action:** **Request a bench appointment**—in Fleurier or by video—to inspect the watch and its dossier with a watchmaker.

The static showcase uses HTML, CSS and minimal JavaScript, relative assets and Google Fonts as its only external requests. Any demonstration enquiry must be clearly identified and must never imply that a message was sent. The eventual appointment experience requires persistent labels, useful error messages, visible focus, generous targets and WCAG 2.2 AA verification.
