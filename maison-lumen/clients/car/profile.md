## 1. **Name and house**

**Veyravel** — an independent maker of electric grand tourers, based near **Saint-Claude in the French Jura**, with its assembly atelier and appointment salon under one roof.

Iria Veyravel, a vehicle vibration engineer, and Lucan Esmeret, a coachbuilder specialising in aluminium interiors, founded the house in 2017. Their starting point was a journey they both made regularly: a full day crossing France and Switzerland with two people and their luggage. They spent five years developing a running prototype around seat comfort, restrained exterior dimensions and access to parts that would eventually need replacing. Patient private funding allowed them to retain one body style and a single engineering programme. The appointment atelier opened in 2026, bringing the driver, upholsterer and engineer into the same commissioning conversation.

## 2. **What they make**

**Veyravel 01:** a purpose-built, battery-electric, two-seat grand touring coupé.

- **Touring proportions:** 4.78 metres long and 1.89 metres wide, excluding mirrors; a rear hatch and 360 litres of luggage capacity behind the seats. The luggage compartment accommodates two fitted cases and two weekend bags.
- **Measured performance:** two motors, all-wheel drive and a target combined output of 450 kW. Suspension development prioritises control over broken surfaces and sustained motorway comfort.
- **Electric architecture:** 800 volts, 104 kWh usable battery capacity and a target combined WLTP range of 520 kilometres. Target DC charging is 10–80% in 27 minutes with a conditioned battery and suitable charger; published data must include the full charging curve and test conditions.
- **Personal fitting:** adjustable seat padding, steering-wheel reach and pedal positioning within validated limits; physical climate and demisting controls. Wool upholstery, leather contact surfaces and satin aluminium trim are individually replaceable.
- **Production and purchase:** a house-wide ceiling of **180 cars annually**, sold directly **by appointment**. Indicative commissioning price: **€480,000 before taxes**, with a written specification and delivery window agreed after the private drive.

## 3. **Niche and audience**

**The habitual continental traveller.** A founder, architect or family-business principal, typically 45–65, who divides time between a European city and a country or mountain home. They drive themselves, usually with one companion, and buy the car for repeated journeys of several hundred kilometres. Their garage already contains a comfortable SUV, an electric executive saloon and an older sports coupé; they understand both charging and the compromises of collectible cars. Veyravel earns their consideration through manageable dimensions, properly fitted seats, useful luggage space and a long appointment drive on roads resembling their own.

**The custodian of contemporary craft.** A design collector or second-generation entrepreneur, typically 35–55, who commissions furniture, owns independent mechanical watches and keeps purchases for decades. They already own a restored combustion grand tourer and a practical everyday EV. They want an original contemporary automobile with identifiable makers and a credible future maintenance path. Their deciding evidence is access to the workshop, a complete build dossier, replaceable interior components and written arrangements for parts, diagnostics and battery servicing.

## 5. **What sets them apart**

1. **The commission begins with the driver’s body and intended journeys.** Each owner completes a three-hour fitting and road session before choosing finishes. **Proof required:** the signed fitting record, documented adjustment limits and a repeat drive demonstrating the agreed changes; owners receive their final settings and material samples.

2. **Touring claims come with usable evidence.** The development programme includes luggage-loaded motorway driving, rough secondary roads, winter conditions and repeated charging stops. **Proof required:** independently witnessed route reports recording temperature, speed, load, consumption, charging time and cabin noise, alongside the eventual homologated figures. A prospect can inspect the methodology and reproduce the luggage fit.

3. **Continued service is designed into the product and purchase contract.** Battery modules are accessible to qualified technicians, upholstery sections can be replaced separately, and core driving functions operate without a cloud account. The ownership agreement commits to parts and diagnostic support for 15 years after the final 01 is built. **Proof required:** a witnessed module replacement, priced service procedures, supplier agreements, diagnostic access arrangements and an independently administered maintenance reserve with annual reporting.

These are evidence requirements for the fictional business, not claims of completed real-world testing.

**Homepage sections, in order:**

3. **Fit:** the seat, steering and pedal fitting process, with a maker’s portrait.
4. **Materials and assembly:** wool, leather and aluminium photographed beside the components they become.
5. **Engineering evidence:** clearly labelled development targets and links to available test methods and results.
6. **Continued ownership:** an illustrated service-access detail and a concise explanation of the support agreement.
7. **The house:** the founders, the Jura atelier and its commissioning practice.
8. **Private appointment:** what happens during the visit, its duration and the next step, followed by direct contact details.

**The one action:** **Request a private appointment.** Repeat that wording in the header, hero and closing section. The appointment office arranges a fitting and extended drive; the website does not accept deposits.
