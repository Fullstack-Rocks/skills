# Accessibility for Luxury Websites: WCAG 2.2 AA & Quiet Luxury

## 1. Context & Positioning for Maison Lumen

In [BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md#L1-L33), Maison Lumen defines its aesthetic as **quiet luxury**: *"Restraint over spectacle, craft over campaigns, and proof over adjectives. Every client site has to feel like walking into the house itself, and still meet WCAG 2.2 AA."*

Accessibility is the digital equivalent of architectural hospitality. Just as a physical Paris or Milan flagship does not force a guest through a heavy door without assistance or print a horology catalogue in unreadable ink, a digital flagship for our own agency and our three showcase clients—an **electric grand tourer automaker**, an **independent haute horology house**, and an **invitation-only private estate** ([BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md#L13-L16))—must treat [W3C WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) (published as a W3C Recommendation on October 5, 2023) as a mark of craft rather than a regulatory compromise.

## 2. How WCAG 2.2 AA Hits Luxury Aesthetics

### Low-Contrast Thin Type
* **The Conflict:** Luxury sites frequently pair hairline serif or geometric sans-serif cuts (`font-weight: 100–200`) with muted taupe, champagne, or warm grey text on off-white or charcoal backgrounds. Ultra-thin strokes suffer from sub-pixel anti-aliasing drop-off on standard-DPI screens and fail [WCAG SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum), which mandates at least **4.5:1** contrast for normal text and **3:1** for large text (`≥18pt` / `24px` regular, or `≥14pt` / `18.66px` bold). Furthermore, [SC 1.4.4 Resize Text](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#resize-text) and [SC 1.4.12 Text Spacing](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#text-spacing) require layouts not to clip or overlap when text is zoomed to 200% or when line-height (`1.5`), letter-spacing (`0.12em`), and word-spacing (`0.16em`) are increased.
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Carry restraint through generous whitespace, editorial grid proportions, and unhurried pacing rather than washed-out ink. Use `font-weight: 400` minimum for body copy at `≥4.5:1` contrast (ideally `≥7:1` for prolonged reading), and reserve delicate weights (`300`) strictly for display headings `≥24px` that clear `4.5:1`.

### Text Over Imagery
* **The Conflict:** Full-bleed photography of coachbuilt car interiors, hand-chamfered watch bridges, or sunlit limestone villas often has variable luminance, causing overlaid white or dark typography to pass contrast on one part of the photo and fail [SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum) as the viewport resizes. Baking text into raster images is also forbidden by [SC 1.4.5 Images of Text](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#images-of-text) and [BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md#L32).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Prefer split or stacked editorial compositions (caption below or beside the plate, like an exhibition catalogue) over text-on-photo. When text must sit over imagery, enforce a deterministic CSS scrim (`linear-gradient`) or solid surface token behind the text container so the worst-case background pixel still clears `4.5:1` (verified with the [WebAIM Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/)).

### Dark Themes
* **The Conflict:** Haute horology and automotive flagships favour obsidian or midnight palettes. Pure `#FFFFFF` text on `#000000` (21:1) can cause halation (astigmatism blooming) for users with visual impairments, while secondary labels and hairline input borders (`#2A2A2A` on `#111111`) routinely fail both [SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum) and [SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast) (**3:1** required for UI component boundaries and state indicators).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Use warm mineral darks (`#0E0E10` or `#141311`) with warm alabaster text (`#F2EFE9`, ~16:1 contrast), muted stone secondary text (`#A8A298`, ~6.8:1 contrast), and subtle brass/stone borders that maintain `≥3:1` against adjacent surfaces.

### Autoplay Video and Motion
* **The Conflict:** Atmospheric looping hero films (a grand tourer gliding through alpine passes, a tourbillon cage rotating, waves lapping a private cove) violate [SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide) (Level A) if they play automatically for more than 5 seconds without a visible mechanism to pause, stop, or hide them. If audio plays for more than 3 seconds, [SC 1.4.2 Audio Control](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#audio-control) is violated. Parallax and scroll-triggered zoom effects also trigger vestibular disorders ([SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#animation-from-interactions), AAA best practice; explicitly required in [BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md#L32)).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Keep all ambient video `muted`, include a persistent, keyboard-accessible `<button>` ("Pause film" / "Play film") with `≥3:1` contrast, and honour `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` by halting autoplay in JS (`video.pause()`), disabling scroll-linked transforms, and displaying a curated high-resolution `poster` still.

### Custom Cursors and Scroll-Jacking
* **The Conflict:** Agency showpieces (especially on [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/)) frequently hide the native OS cursor (`cursor: none`) to render a lagging JS circle, and hijack wheel/touch events with inertia libraries. Hiding the OS cursor breaks assistive screen magnifiers and custom high-visibility OS pointers. Scroll-jacking breaks `Space` / `PageDown` / arrow-key scrolling ([SC 2.1.1 Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard)), causes motion sickness, and can trap focus ([SC 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap)).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Never set `cursor: none` and never hijack native document scroll. Rely on native browser scrolling (`scroll-behavior: smooth` only inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`) and understated CSS hover transitions on elements themselves.

### Carousels & Editorial Galleries
* **The Conflict:** Lookbook sliders and watch-calibre galleries often rely solely on swipe/drag gestures, auto-advance without pause buttons, hide off-screen slides improperly from keyboard/screen-reader users, or use tiny 6px pagination dots.
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Follow the [W3C WAI Carousel Tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/carousels/): prefer static asymmetric editorial grids over carousels wherever possible. Where a gallery is essential, disable auto-rotation by default, provide explicit "Previous" and "Next" `<button>` controls (satisfying [SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements) and [SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum)), wrap the gallery in `<section aria-roledescription="carousel" aria-label="...">`, and manage `inert` or `tabindex` on hidden slides.

### Focus Visibility
* **The Conflict:** `outline: none` is the single most common CSS anti-pattern on luxury sites because designers dislike the default browser blue ring. Removing it violates [SC 2.4.7 Focus Visible](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible) (Level AA) and [SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Design focus rings as intentional architectural detailing using `:focus-visible`—for example, a `2px solid` high-contrast border in warm ivory or burnished gold (`≥3:1` against background) with `outline-offset: 4px`, or a dual-ring (`box-shadow: 0 0 0 2px var(--bg), 0 0 0 4px var(--fg)`) that remains legible over any image or surface, aligned with the [WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.13 Focus Appearance](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-appearance) benchmark.

### Alt Text for Art-Directed Imagery
* **The Conflict:** Luxury sites either omit `alt` attributes entirely (falling back to filenames like `IMG_8842_v3.webp`), stuff them with marketing clichés, or write verbose descriptions on purely atmospheric textures—violating [SC 1.1.1 Non-text Content](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-content).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Follow the [W3C WAI Images Decision Tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/):
  * **Informative / Craft Proof Images:** Describe the material, finish, and form concisely and factually (e.g., `alt="Three-quarter profile of the Aethelgard GT in brushed titanium grey, showing flush pillarless glass and forged 21-inch aero wheels"` or `alt="Macro view of Calibre 04 hand-bevelled German silver bridges and free-sprung balance wheel"`).
  * **Purely Decorative Textures / Dividers:** Use empty `alt=""` (and `aria-hidden="true"` on decorative inline `<svg>`) so screen readers are not cluttered with noise.

### Forms (Private Appointment Booking & Concierge Inquiries)
* **The Conflict:** Salon appointment forms, test-drive requests, and private residence inquiries frequently use minimalist bottom-border inputs with disappearing `placeholder` text instead of persistent `<label>` elements, low-contrast bottom rules (`<3:1`), and colour-only error states—failing [SC 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#labels-or-instructions), [SC 1.3.1 Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships), [SC 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#identify-input-purpose), and [SC 3.3.1 Error Identification](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#error-identification).
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** Always render visible, permanent `<label for="...">` elements above each field. Ensure input borders have `≥3:1` contrast against the page background ([SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)). Include standard `autocomplete` attributes (`name`, `email`, `tel`). Use `<fieldset>` and `<legend>` for radio groups (e.g., preferred boutique city: Paris, Milan, Geneva). On validation error, set `aria-invalid="true"`, connect a descriptive message via `aria-describedby` ("Please enter a valid telephone number including country code"), and avoid custom JS date-pickers unless they implement full [WAI-ARIA Date Picker Dialog](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/examples/datepicker-dialog/) keyboard navigation.

### Cookie Banners & Modals
* **The Conflict:** Cookie consent bars and newsletter modals on luxury sites either fail to trap keyboard focus when modal, or sit as a non-modal fixed bar at the bottom of the viewport while the user tabs through background links hidden underneath the banner—a direct violation of [WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum). Note that in Maison Lumen's static showcase ([BRIEF.md](../BRIEF.md#L30)), there are no third-party tracking scripts, so omitting tracking banners altogether is the cleanest luxury choice; when built for production client flagships, they must be engineered properly.
* **The Quiet-Luxury Solution:** If a consent banner is present, either make it a true modal `<dialog>` (`aria-modal="true"`, focus moved inside on open, `Escape` closes/rejects, background set to `inert`) or place it first in the DOM order with `scroll-padding-bottom` matching its height so focused elements are never obscured. Give "Reject non-essential" equal visual weight and contrast as "Accept" (aligning with EU GDPR / CNIL dark-pattern rules and WCAG contrast).

## 3. The WCAG 2.2 Additions & Their Impact on Luxury Sites

[What's New in WCAG 2.2 (W3C WAI)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/) introduced nine new success criteria (and removed [4.1.1 Parsing](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#parsing)). The key AA and A additions every Maison Lumen flagship must pass are:

1. **[SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) (Level AA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum):** When any UI component receives keyboard focus, it must not be entirely hidden by author-created content. Fixed transparent/glassmorphic luxury headers, floating "Book an Appointment" bars, and sticky cookie banners require matching `scroll-padding-top` and `scroll-padding-bottom` on `html` so tabbed elements scroll clear of sticky chrome.
2. **[SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) (Level AA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum):** Pointer targets must be at least **24×24 CSS pixels** (or have a 24px spacing circle around their centre), except for inline text links. Minimalist luxury UI controls—carousel dots, watch-dial colour swatches, audio mute icons, modal `×` buttons, and footer social icons—must have a minimum `24×24px` hit box (and ideally `44×44px` on touch viewports per [SC 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced)).
3. **[SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (Level AA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements):** Any action that uses dragging must be operable by a single pointer without dragging. Interactive 360° vehicle rotators, before/after craftsmanship sliders, and horizontal timeline scrubbers must include single-click/tap buttons (e.g., `<button>Rotate left 15°</button>`, `<button>Next chapter</button>`).
4. **[SC 3.2.6 Consistent Help (Level A)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#consistent-help):** Help mechanisms repeated across pages (private concierge phone number, salon email, live client advisor link) must appear in the **same relative DOM order** on every page.
5. **[SC 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum) (Level AA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#accessible-authentication-minimum):** Logging into a private client portal or invitation-only estate booking area must not require a cognitive function test (memorising a password or solving an object-recognition CAPTCHA) unless it supports password managers (`autocomplete="current-password"` / `one-time-code`), copy-pasting into Passcode/OTP fields, WebAuthn/Passkeys, or magic email links.
6. **[SC 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (Level A)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#redundant-entry):** Multi-step concierge or bespoke commissioning flows must not ask the client to re-type information already entered in the same session.

## 4. Real Luxury Brands & Agencies: Who Does It Well or Badly

* **Porsche — [Porsche Design System v3 Accessibility Statement](https://designsystem.porsche.com/v3/must-know/accessibility/statement/) & [Introduction](https://designsystem.porsche.com/v3/must-know/accessibility/introduction/) (Done Well):** Porsche builds WCAG 2.2 AA directly into its tokenized component library (`p-carousel`, `p-modal`, `p-pin-code`, `p-button-pure`), enforcing high-contrast dark/light themes, 200% text zoom resilience, and full keyboard operability while preserving unmistakable automotive prestige.
* **Apple — [Apple Accessibility](https://www.apple.com/accessibility/) & Apple Watch Hermès (Done Well):** Demonstrates that ultra-premium art direction, dark-mode product storytelling, and motion-rich hardware showcases can coexist with visible play/pause controls on every inline video, strict `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` fallbacks, and clean VoiceOver semantics.
* **Hermès — [Hermès Finance Accessibility Statement (RGAA 4.0 / Article 47 Audit)](https://finance.hermes.com/en/accessibility/) (Transparent Audit of Luxury Pitfalls):** Audited by Ipedis (updated October 2025), Hermès transparently reports a 62.12% compliance rate (41 compliant, 25 non-compliant criteria) and catalogues the exact defects that plague luxury web builds: decorative images not ignored by screen readers, insufficient text contrast, scripts inoperable by keyboard, missing visible keyboard focus, and text-spacing overrides clipping content.
* **Chanel ([Chanel US Accessibility Page](https://www.chanel.com/us/accessibility_page/)) & Cartier ([Cartier Accessibility Statement](https://www.cartier.com/en-gr/cartier-accessibility-statement)) (Mixed / Remediated Under Pressure):** Both houses have invested in dedicated accessibility statements, high-contrast toggles, and assistive partnerships (such as Level Access at Cartier) after years of industry-wide U.S. litigation targeting luxury fashion e-commerce ([CFDA Legal Dispatch on ADA Web Accessibility Litigation](https://cfda.com/news/legal-dispatch-ada-web-accessibility-litigation-presents-high-risk-to-fashion-companies/)).
* **Award-Winning Interactive Studios ([Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/), [Active Theory](https://activetheory.net/), [Resn](https://resn.co.nz/) vs. [AREA 17](https://area17.com/) & [Work & Co](https://work.co/)):** While WebGL-heavy campaign studios on [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/) push visual spectacle, heavy scroll-jacking, canvas-rendered text, and custom cursors routinely fail WCAG 2.2 AA. By contrast, editorial and product-focused agencies like [AREA 17](https://area17.com/) and [Work & Co](https://work.co/) demonstrate that structured semantic HTML, typographic hierarchy, and resilient component systems age far better for cultural and luxury institutions.

## 5. Legal Context: European Accessibility Act (EAA 2025) & US ADA

* **European Accessibility Act — [Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng):** Enforceable across all 27 EU member states since **June 28, 2025**, the EAA mandates that all e-commerce websites, booking services, and digital consumer platforms serving EU clients meet harmonized accessibility standards mapped via **EN 301 549** ([ETSI EN 301 549 V3.2.1](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/03.02.01_60/en_301549v030201p.pdf), aligning with WCAG 2.1/2.2 Level AA). Only microenterprises (`<10` employees and `<€2M` turnover) are exempt. For a Paris/Milan agency like Maison Lumen, compliance is mandatory under French ([RGAA / Article 47 Law No. 2005-102](https://design.numerique.gouv.fr/accessibilite-numerique/rgaa/)) and Italian ([AgID Stanca Law](https://www.agid.gov.it/it/design-servizi/accessibilita)) market surveillance.
* **United States ADA Title III — [DOJ Guidance on Web Accessibility and the ADA](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/):** U.S. federal and state courts (especially New York and California) enforce website accessibility under Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act as "places of public accommodation." According to [UsableNet's Year-End Digital Accessibility Lawsuit Report](https://info.usablenet.com/hubfs/Remediated%20-%202025_Year-End_Digital_Accessibility_Lawsuit_Report_FINAL.pdf), over 4,000 digital lawsuits are filed annually, with **retail and e-commerce accounting for ~70% of all claims**, and luxury fashion/jewelry houses among the most frequently sued verticals ([CFDA](https://cfda.com/news/legal-dispatch-ada-web-accessibility-litigation-presents-high-risk-to-fashion-companies/)).
* **Why Overlays Fail:** Quick-fix JavaScript "accessibility overlay" widgets do not provide legal immunity or technical compliance. In 2024–2025, hundreds of ADA lawsuits targeted sites actively running overlay widgets ([UsableNet Report](https://info.usablenet.com/hubfs/Remediated%20-%202025_Year-End_Digital_Accessibility_Lawsuit_Report_FINAL.pdf)), and over 800 accessibility specialists have signed the [Overlay Fact Sheet](https://overlayfactsheet.com/en/) documenting how overlays interfere with native screen readers. Native semantic HTML and CSS are the only defensible standard.

## 6. Reviewer Checklist for Maison Lumen & Client Sites

Reviewers (`reviews/<slug>.md`) can audit every site against this 12-point WCAG 2.2 AA checklist:

- [ ] **1. Text Contrast ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)):** All body copy (`<24px` regular / `<18.66px` bold) measures `≥4.5:1` against its exact background; large display type (`≥24px`) measures `≥3:1` (recommended `≥4.5:1` if weight is `300`).
- [ ] **2. Text Over Media ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum), [1.4.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#images-of-text)):** Zero text is baked into raster images (logos excepted). Any text over photography/video has a CSS scrim or surface guarantee so the lightest/darkest underlying pixel still clears `4.5:1`.
- [ ] **3. Non-Text Contrast ([SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)):** Form borders, buttons, custom radio/select controls, and focus indicators maintain `≥3:1` contrast against adjacent surfaces in both light and dark sections.
- [ ] **4. Reflow & Text Spacing ([SC 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#resize-text), [1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#reflow), [1.4.12](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#text-spacing)):** At `320px` CSS width (400% zoom) and with WCAG text-spacing overrides applied, no text is clipped (`overflow: hidden` on fixed-height boxes) and no horizontal scrollbar appears.
- [ ] **5. Motion & Autoplay Video ([SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide), [2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#animation-from-interactions)):** Every autoplaying video is muted and has a visible, keyboard-operable Pause/Play `<button>`. Enabling `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` halts autoplay videos and disables parallax/scroll transitions.
- [ ] **6. Native Pointer & Scroll ([SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard), [2.1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap)):** `cursor: none` is never used; native browser scrolling (`Space`, `PageDown`, `Up/Down` arrows, trackpad) is unintercepted.
- [ ] **7. Focus Visibility & Not Obscured ([SC 2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible), [2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum)):** Tabbing from top to bottom shows a high-contrast (`≥3:1`) `:focus-visible` indicator on every interactive element, include a "Skip to main content" link ([SC 2.4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#bypass-blocks)), and sticky headers/footers never obscure the focused element.
- [ ] **8. Target Size ([SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum)):** Every standalone interactive control (buttons, gallery arrows, swatches, checkboxes) has a computed click/tap area of at least `24×24px` (or `24px` radial spacing).
- [ ] **9. Single-Pointer Alternatives to Dragging ([SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements)):** Every carousel, horizontal gallery, or interactive comparison slider can be operated via single-click `<button>` controls without dragging.
- [ ] **10. Art-Directed Alt Text ([SC 1.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-content)):** All informative `<img>` elements have specific, craft-accurate `alt` descriptions; purely decorative images use `alt=""` and decorative `<svg>` elements use `aria-hidden="true"`.
- [ ] **11. Accessible Forms ([SC 1.3.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships), [1.3.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#identify-input-purpose), [3.3.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#error-identification), [3.3.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#labels-or-instructions), [3.3.8](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#accessible-authentication-minimum)):** Every input has a persistent visible `<label for="...">`, valid `autocomplete` token, `≥3:1` border contrast, programmatic error messages (`aria-invalid` + `aria-describedby`), and supports paste/autofill.
- [ ] **12. Semantic Landmarks, Headings & Consistent Help ([SC 1.3.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships), [2.4.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#page-titled), [3.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#language-of-page), [3.2.6](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#consistent-help)):** Valid `<html lang="...">`, descriptive `<title>`, `<header>`, `<main id="main">`, `<nav>`, `<footer>`, sequential `<h1>`–`<h3>` hierarchy without skipped levels, and consistent concierge contact placement.

## 7. What Maison Lumen Should Do (8 Concrete, Testable Recommendations)

1. **Enforce a contrast-verified CSS color token floor ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum), [1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)):** Define all foreground, muted-text, and border colors as CSS custom properties in `:root` with pre-audited contrast ratios: body text `≥7:1`, secondary captions `≥4.5:1`, and hairline rules/input borders `≥3:1` against their surface token. *Test:* Run automated contrast checks and verify zero text elements fall below `4.5:1` (`3:1` for `≥24px`).
2. **Ban sub-400 font weights on body copy and enforce deterministic scrims on hero media ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)):** Restrict `font-weight: 300` to headings `≥24px` (`1.5rem`) and forbid `font-weight: 100–200` entirely. Whenever text overlays hero imagery, apply a CSS gradient scrim or solid backdrop plate so contrast against the worst-case image pixel remains `≥4.5:1`. *Test:* Disable images or replace them with solid `#FFFFFF` and `#000000` test blocks in DevTools; overlaid text must still pass `4.5:1`.
3. **Standardize a dual-ring `:focus-visible` indicator and `scroll-padding-top` across all four sites ([SC 2.4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#bypass-blocks), [2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible), [2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum)):** Add a first-child `<a class="skip-link" href="#main">Skip to main content</a>` and style `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--focus-color); outline-offset: 4px; }` with `≥3:1` contrast, plus `html { scroll-padding-top: var(--header-height, 5rem); }`. *Test:* Tab through the entire page from the URL bar; every interactive control must show a clear ring and never hide behind a sticky header.
4. **Gate all motion and autoplay media behind `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` and explicit Pause buttons ([SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide), [2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#animation-from-interactions)):** Every ambient video must include a visible `<button type="button" aria-pressed="false">Pause motion</button>` and automatically pause on load when `window.matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches` is true, while CSS disables all transitions/animations. *Test:* Emulate `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` in browser DevTools and confirm zero animations or playing videos run.
5. **Forbid `cursor: none`, scroll-jacking, and drag-only galleries ([SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard), [2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements)):** Keep native OS cursors and native browser scrolling intact on all pages. Prefer static asymmetric editorial grids for lookbooks, watch calibres, and estate galleries; if a carousel or slider is used, provide single-click `<button>` controls ("Previous", "Next") alongside keyboard arrow support. *Test:* Grep CSS/JS for `cursor: none` and `wheel`/`touchmove` `preventDefault()`; operate all galleries with a single click or keyboard `Tab` + `Enter`.
